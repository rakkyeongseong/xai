Explainable AI for Brane Tilings

Diagnosing Inconsistencies in 4d N = 1 Gauge Theories with Explainable AI · Seong-Jin Lee, Rak-Kyeong Seong · arXiv:2609.21867

Brane tilings are bipartite graphs on a 2-torus that encode 4-dimensional N=1 supersymmetric gauge theories arising on D3-branes probing toric Calabi-Yau 3-folds. Only those satisfying geometric consistency conditions correspond to well-behaved quantum field theories.

This page carries the family studied in the paper: diagonal edges added to the hexagonal faces of the brane tiling for the abelian orbifold C3/(Z3×Z3) with orbifold action (0,1,2)(1,0,2). Click a hexagon to add or change its diagonal and the four panels below recompute — the tiling, the saliency map of the trained convolutional network, the toric diagram with its consistency verdict, and the quiver of the gauge theory with its superpotential.

The laboratory runs entirely in the browser. The permanent of the Kasteleyn matrix is evaluated exactly, the consistency criterion is checked in integer arithmetic, and the network's forward pass and input gradient are computed from the manuscript checkpoint itself, so what you see is the published model's own explanation rather than a reconstruction of it.

Below the laboratory, the paper's results are laid out in full: how the network was trained, how it classifies all 262,144 tilings, how well its saliency finds a defect in each of the 7,236 1-edge-fixable ones, and the three worked examples, each a click away from the laboratory. Those numbers are not computed in the browser: they are read from the companion code's evaluation of the same checkpoint and from the record of its training run.

        How to read the panels
        
                    
        From bipartite graph to gauge theory
        
                
        
                
        
                    
        
                        
        
                            
        (a) The brane tiling
        
                            
        The base tiling is the periodic honeycomb of the orbifold: nine white and nine black nodes in the fundamental domain, nine hexagonal faces and 27 edges. Faces are gauge groups, edges are bifundamental chiral fields, and nodes are superpotential terms.
        
                            
        Each hexagon may carry one long diagonal in one of three orientations, encoded by a digit 0–3. Reading the nine digits row by row as a base-4 number gives the configuration index, so the family has 49 = 262,144 members. A diagonal splits a hexagon into two quadrilaterals, adding one gauge group.
        
                        
        
                    
        
                    
        
                        
        
                            
        (b) The CNN and its saliency map
        
                            
        A three-layer convolutional network with circular padding reads the 9×9×9 Kasteleyn tensor and returns one logit for the consistent class. The weights are the manuscript checkpoint, evaluated here in JavaScript.
        
                            
        Gradient saliency is projected to a node-pair map for display. For a 1-edge-fixable tiling, the present diagonal with the largest saliency component is the network's diagnosis; removing it, by Higgsing the corresponding chiral field, restores consistency in 88.18% of the 7,236 cases against a matched random baseline of 21.10%.
        
                        
        
                    
        
                    
        
                        
        
                            
        (c) Toric diagram and consistency
        
                            
        The Kasteleyn matrix K(x, y) weights every edge by its winding numbers around the torus. Its permanent is the Newton polynomial P(x, y), whose Newton polygon is the toric diagram; each lattice point is labelled by the number of perfect matchings carrying that monomial.
        
                            
        A tiling is geometrically consistent when 2A = NG: twice the area of the toric diagram equals the number of gauge groups. This is the paper's criterion, taken there as equivalent to the existence of an isoradial embedding and to properly ordered, non-self-intersecting zig-zag paths. Only 1,387 of the 262,144 tilings pass. Of the failures, 7,236 are one diagonal deletion away from consistency, the paper's 1-edge-fixable tilings; counting the fewest deletions in the same way, 39,006 are 3-edge-fixable, needing three and no fewer.
        
                        
        
                    
        
                    
        
                        
        
                            
        (d) The quiver and its superpotential
        
                            
        Each face of the tiling is a gauge group U(N)i and each edge a bifundamental chiral field Xij, drawn as an arrow from the face on one side of the edge to the face on the other, clockwise around the white node of the edge. For the bare tiling this is the quiver of the orbifold: the nine faces fall into three classes, and every gauge group has one field to each group of the next class. Following the group's brane tiling figures, six gauge groups sit on a hexagon and three inside it.
        
                            
        A diagonal splits a gauge group in two, joined by the diagonal's own field; Higgsing that field, which is what Remove this edge does, joins them again. The superpotential has one term per node of the tiling: the fields around a white node, clockwise, with a plus sign, and around a black node, anticlockwise, with a minus sign, so that every field appears once with each sign.

        Training the classifier

The network is given only the binary consistency label, never the location of a defect.

        Network and schedule
        
                        
        
                        
        
                            
        
                                
        Input

        Network

        Reading the numbers
        
                        
        
                        
        
                            
        Because 260,757 of the tilings are inconsistent, calling every tiling inconsistent would already score 99.47% accuracy. The F1-score on the consistent class is the informative number.
        
                            
        The evaluation covers the whole family, including the 15,257 tilings of the training pool, so it shows how the network partitions the family rather than how it generalises; there is no separate test split.
        
                        
        
                    
        
                
        
            
        
        
        
        
        
            
        
                
        
                    
        Defect localisation
        
                    
        Finding the defect in 7,236 tilings
        
                    
        A diagonal is a defect when removing it alone restores consistency, and a tiling with at least one is 1-edge-fixable: 5,157 of them have a single defect and 2,079 have two. The 729 components Suvk of each saliency tensor are ranked, and Top-k succeeds when a defect is among the k highest.
        
                
        
                
        
                    
        
                        
        
                            
        Top-k accuracy by what is ranked
        
                        
        
                        
        
                            
        Loading the results…
        
                            
        Ranking only the diagonals present in the tiling lifts Top-1 from 82.92% to 88.18%, more than four times the matched random baseline: a present diagonal picked at random is a defect with probability NR/ND, 21.10% on average over the tilings. That restricted choice is the edge Remove this edge deletes in the laboratory.
        
                        
        
                    
        
                    
        
                        
        
                            
        Where the top component lands
        
                        
        
                        
        
                            
        Loading the results…
        
                            
        Over all 729 components, the top one is a diagonal of the tiling in 6,667 tilings and a component that carries no edge in the other 569; it is never a base edge. For tilings with six diagonals, the top component carries no edge in 15.12% of cases against 7.86% overall, one reason ND = 6 has the lowest Top-1 accuracy over all 729 components.
        
                        
        
                    
        
                
        
                
        
                    
        
                        
        Top-1 accuracy by the number of diagonals
        
                        Table II · Fig. 5
                    
        
                    
        
                        
        Loading the results…
        
                        
        Accuracy is not monotonic in ND. It broadly follows the mean defect fraction, the share of a tiling's diagonals that are defects: six and nine diagonals have the smallest share of correct candidates and the lowest Top-1 accuracies, though the defect is often still near the top of the ranking. The number of defects does not explain it, since every tiling with one diagonal and every tiling with six has a single defect, yet over all 729 components the two rows sit at opposite ends. No tiling with two or three diagonals is 1-edge-fixable. The bars count the tilings with each ND: 6.43 diagonals on average, with a median of 7.

        Provenance
        
                    
        Where these numbers come from
        
                
        
                
        
                    
        Every number in the results sections is read from model/results.json, which tools/export_results.py writes from two sources. The classification and localisation results come from a fresh evaluation of the manuscript checkpoint by the companion code, the Python code that accompanies the paper: inference on all 262,144 tilings and saliency gradients for all 7,236 1-edge-fixable ones, with no cached predictions. That evaluation passes 501 of the verifier's 501 numerical and geometric checks against the companion code's reference values, among them Tables I and II of the paper. The training curves are the history recorded while the checkpoint was trained, read as it was saved. The laboratory runs the same network in the browser, and its test suite matches the Python saliency to about 10−7 of the peak.
